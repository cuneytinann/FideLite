Chess960 · Castling Skeletons

56 skeletons. 960 positions.

Castling in Fischer-random chess depends only on where the king and its two rooks sit, and the king always stands between the rooks — so across all 960 setups there are exactly C(8,3)=56 king–rook skeletons. Each card holds one skeleton and every Scharnagl ID built on it. Ordered by file, from R K R on a–b–c through f–g–h. All generated live.

R K R skeleton · Scharnagl IDs

Skeleton written R K R with dashes for empty files (e.g. -R--K-R-). #518 (RNBQKBNR, the orthodox start) is highlighted where it appears. Numbering after Reinhard Scharnagl (FRC/Chess960, 2004) — the same IDs FIDE uses.

Why 18 or 12: the five files a skeleton leaves free split either 3–2 or 4–1 by square colour. A 3–2 split allows 3×2 bishop placements and 3 squares for the queen — 18 positions. In the eight 4–1 skeletons one bishop is pinned to the lone minority file: 1×4×3 = 12.
